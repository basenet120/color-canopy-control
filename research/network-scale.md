# network-scale

**HEADLINE:** 200 Hubspace bulbs in one 34x37 ft room is solvable with 4-6 low-power APs at ~35-50 clients per 2.4GHz radio on a WPA2-only/20MHz/channels-1-6-11 network — the real bottlenecks are Hubspace's strictly one-bulb-at-a-time app+QR provisioning (no bulk claiming, ~7-17 labor-hours for 200 units) and Afero cloud rate limits, not raw Wi-Fi capacity.

## Findings
- [HIGH] **Hubspace A19 bulbs are 2.4GHz-only Wi-Fi (802.11 b/g/n class) with Bluetooth LE required for setup — confirmed by the official EcoSmart use-and-care guide**
  - The official manual for the A19 RGBW bulb (model 12A19060WRGBWH1, Item 1008 588 501, FCC ID 2AB2Q-12A800STQT) states 'This Hubspace bulb requires a 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network' and 'the Hubspace app will only show Wi-Fi networks compatible with this bulb' (it filters out 5GHz SSIDs), and 'Bluetooth access is required for product setup' with the phone within BLE range of the bulb. The manual carries Wi-Fi CERTIFIED and Bluetooth logos. No 5GHz support of any kind.
  - src: https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/c2/c289878d-e597-4484-82a4-0819d7060c0c.pdf
- [HIGH] **There is NO bulk/batch provisioning: every Hubspace bulb must be added individually in the mobile app, with QR-code scan as the fastest path; each QR is unique to one device and doubles as a typeable numeric code**
  - Official manual: 'Each smart Hubspace bulb must be added individually by scanning the QR code or by following the instructions for manual set up... Scanning the QR code is the fastest method... QR code must be scanned from inside the Hubspace app.' The QR is printed on the bulb housing AND on the boxed Quick Start Guide card; 'The QR code are numbers. You can enter those in manually instead of scanning'; 'Can I scan the same QR code to add multiple products? No. Each product has a unique QR code.' If no QR is available, a manual flow (power on bulb, tap +, Search button) discovers it over BLE. Plan physical logistics: keep each bulb's quick-start card or photograph the bulb-neck QR before installing bulbs 10 ft up in the canopy grid — scanning QRs on installed overhead bulbs will be miserable.
  - src: https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/c2/c289878d-e597-4484-82a4-0819d7060c0c.pdf
- [HIGH] **Moving a device between Hubspace accounts is a documented QR-based re-claim: scanning the QR from the new account 'will transfer it to your account' — no factory reset required**
  - The A19 manual troubleshooting table answers 'A product is on another account. How do I transfer it?' with 'Scan the QR code and it will transfer to your account.' This is the sanctioned mechanism for redistributing the 200 canopy bulbs plus vignette devices across the separate per-vignette accounts Home Depot recommends, and for post-show asset handling. Untested at scale and unclear whether Wi-Fi credentials survive the transfer (bench-verify); also implies physical QR possession equals ownership, so guard the quick-start cards.
  - src: https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/c2/c289878d-e597-4484-82a4-0819d7060c0c.pdf
- [MEDIUM] **No official factory-reset procedure is published in the A19 manual; the community-standard reset for Hubspace bulbs is a rapid power on/off cycle (3-5 flicks) until the bulb flashes**
  - The official manual's troubleshooting only offers Bluetooth off/on, phone restart, and re-scan — no reset sequence. Third-party guidance for Hubspace bulbs describes flicking the switch on/off 3-5 times until the bulb blinks to reset, and the generic smart-bulb pattern (on 10s, then 5x off/on 2s cycles until flash) applies to most Wi-Fi bulbs. Treat the exact sequence as unverified until bench-tested on the production SKU; also note that in a 40-bulb zone on one breaker, a power-cycle 'reset' pattern could accidentally reset an entire zone — resets should be done per-socket, another argument for per-zone switched circuits with individual lamp control during commissioning.
  - src: https://whizz-experts.com/support/smart-devices/smart-light-not-connecting-wifi/ and https://www.creeledrevolution.com/how-to-reset-a-smart-bulb/
- [HIGH] **The community control stack (aioafero) is control-only — it CANNOT provision/claim devices; onboarding is app-exclusive, and aioafero now supports Afero's 'Conclave' push channel for near-real-time state**
  - aioafero docs describe auth (AferoBridgeV1, refresh tokens), 13 typed device controllers (lights, locks, fans, switches, thermostats, security, etc.) and a 'Conclave push channel' for real-time updates, but no add-device/claiming API — the Hubspace phone app with BLE is the only claiming path. The jdeath Home Assistant integration README confirms: 'Conclave push updates are enabled by default for near real-time device changes. REST polling remains as a true-up (default every 15 minutes / 900 seconds).' This matters for the kiosk bridge: subscribe to Conclave for state, send commands via REST, and do NOT design any provisioning automation around aioafero.
  - src: https://aioafero.readthedocs.io/en/latest/ and https://github.com/jdeath/Hubspace-Homeassistant/blob/main/README.md
- [HIGH] **Afero cloud rate-limits aggressively: a maintained Hubspace HA integration floors device-state refresh at 2 seconds and splits cheap delta polls from expensive full-discovery calls (hourly) specifically because of Afero throttling**
  - The Emkraan/homeassistant-hubspace README documents state polling default 30s with a hard floor: 'Floored at 2s - Afero rate-limits aggressively', device discovery (full metadata) default 3600s, and notes Hubspace is 'cloud_polling — there is no local control path.' A jdeath PR (#262) split polling from discovery 'to reduce bandwidth by up to 91%'. For 5 zones x 40 bulbs, this argues for group-level commands, Conclave push instead of tight polling, and possibly sharding the canopy across accounts if per-account limits bite — no public numeric rate-limit (req/min) is documented anywhere.
  - src: https://github.com/Emkraan/homeassistant-hubspace and https://github.com/jdeath/Hubspace-Homeassistant/pull/262
- [HIGH] **Afero's architecture makes the phone app a literal hub: devices normally speak Wi-Fi direct to Afero Cloud, but the app can proxy control over BLE when Wi-Fi is absent — meaning 200 installed bulbs are also 200 live BLE radios in the room**
  - Afero developer docs: the mobile app 'functions as a hub, securely sending and receiving messages to and from the Afero Cloud on behalf of the smart device' over BLE; hubs connect to cloud 'via Wi-Fi or LTE'. The A19 manual confirms: a product in a no-Wi-Fi location can be used via 'the app on a phone with an internet connection like LTE. The phone must be within Bluetooth range.' Implications: (1) a staff phone with cellular can rescue/control individual bulbs if the venue Wi-Fi dies, one at a time; (2) BLE advertising from ~200 bulbs adds a small 2.4GHz noise floor (BLE ads are microseconds-long on 3 advertising channels that sit between Wi-Fi 1/6/11 — low real impact, worth a spectrum scan at bench).
  - src: https://afero-docs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/SystemOverview/ and https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/c2/c289878d-e597-4484-82a4-0819d7060c0c.pdf
- [MEDIUM] **Hubspace hardware is built by Leedarson (FCC grantee 2AB2Q = Leedarson Lighting Co., Zhangzhou China); a torn-down Hubspace dimmer used an ESP32-based Leedarson LA02301 module with write-protected flash — ESP32-class silicon explains the 2.4GHz-only + BLE combo and WPA2 expectations**
  - The A19 bulb's FCC ID 2AB2Q-12A800STQT resolves to Leedarson Lighting's grantee code (fccid.io/2AB2Q lists their applications; fccid.io/fcc.report block automated fetching so internal photos were not retrievable). An elektroda.com teardown of the Hubspace 500W dimmer identified an ESP32 with the Leedarson LA02301 module, flash write-protected (no OpenBeken/Tasmota path). ESP32 family = 802.11 b/g/n 2.4GHz 20MHz + BLE, WPA2-PSK universally solid, WPA3/PMF support only in newer SDK builds and frequently absent in vendor firmware. Extrapolation from dimmer to bulb is plausible (same ODM) but unconfirmed for the A19 SKU.
  - src: https://www.elektroda.com/rtvforum/topic4050956.html and https://fccid.io/2AB2Q
- [HIGH] **Run the bulb network as WPA2-PSK (AES/CCMP) ONLY with PMF disabled; WPA3-only breaks IoT outright and even WPA2/WPA3 transition mode (which forces PMF-capable behavior) causes join failures on legacy clients**
  - Evan McCann's UniFi guide: WPA2 'more universally supported', PMF 'leave disabled for WPA2 networks'; multiple sources document that WPA3-only or mixed WPA2/WPA3 mode causes smart bulbs to fail at the 'connecting to Wi-Fi' stage, and the standard fix for Hubspace-class gear is a dedicated 2.4GHz WPA2-PSK(AES) SSID. No official Hubspace WPA3 statement exists — the manual is silent on security mode — so WPA2-only is the safe engineering default, with a 5-bulb WPA2/WPA3-transition join test as an optional bench item. Also keep SSID/PSK to plain alphanumerics (8-63 printable ASCII) and do NOT use a hidden SSID: special characters and hidden SSIDs are recurring smart-bulb join killers.
  - src: https://evanmccann.net/blog/2021/11/unifi-advanced-wi-fi-settings and https://reusecomputers.com/blogs/news/smart-device-wi-fi-problems-fixing-wpa3-and-dual-band-issues-with-alexa-smart-bulbs-and-ipads and https://community.tp-link.com/us/home/kb/detail/412692
- [HIGH] **Design anchor for AP count: Cisco's high-density guidance is ~25 clients per radio (50 per dual-band AP) for good QoE, with real-world 50-60 clients/radio sustained at Mobile World Congress and 100/radio only with bandwidth shaping — so 200 bulbs wants 4-6 APs with 2.4GHz enabled, ~35-50 bulbs per radio**
  - Cisco Wireless High Client Density Design Guide and Meraki guidance: plan ~25 clients/radio for QoE; GSMA MWC (1200+ APs) ran 50-60 clients per radio; densest deployments shape to 4-8 Mbps/client to hit 100/radio. Hubspace bulbs are near-zero-throughput clients (tiny MQTT/TLS keepalives + occasional commands), so the binding constraint is association/management overhead, not bandwidth — 40-50 bulbs/radio is comfortably inside proven territory. With only 3 non-overlapping 2.4GHz channels (1/6/11 at 20MHz), the ceiling in ONE room is effectively 3-6 co-located radios: recommend 5-6 APs (two APs may share a channel at opposite corners at minimum TX power), giving 33-40 bulbs per AP plus headroom for the ~10-20 staff/kiosk/vignette clients.
  - src: https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/td/docs/wireless/controller/technotes/8-7/b_wireless_high_client_density_design_guide.html and https://documentation.meraki.com/Wireless/Design_and_Configure/Architecture_and_Best_Practices/Approximating_Maximum_Clients_per_Access_Point
- [HIGH] **Hard association ceilings are far above the design point: Meraki documents 256 clients/radio (Wi-Fi 5 Wave 2) and 512 clients/radio (Wi-Fi 6) as theoretical maxima; Ruckus R550 claims 512 clients/AP with BeamFlex+; UniFi U6 marketing claims 300+ clients/AP — none of these are design targets**
  - Meraki: 'Wi-Fi 5 Wave 2: 256 clients per radio... Wi-Fi 6: 512 clients per radio = 1024 per AP' explicitly labeled theoretical and 'not something we want to design for.' Ruckus R550 datasheet: 'Up to 512 clients per AP', 2.4GHz radio 2x2:2 up to 574 Mbps, BeamFlex+ 64 antenna patterns per band (Ruckus's polarization-diversity antennas have the best reputation for misbehaving cheap IoT clients). UniFi U6 line: '300+ client' claims (U6-Pro '350+'). Practical takeaway: a single premium AP could technically associate all 200 bulbs, but airtime contention, DHCP/TLS reconnect storms, and single-point-of-failure risk make 4-6 mid-tier APs (U6-Pro/U6+, Omada EAP670, or Ruckus R550/R650 class) the right architecture; UniFi is the budget-sane choice, Ruckus the premium de-risk choice.
  - src: https://documentation.meraki.com/Wireless/Design_and_Configure/Architecture_and_Best_Practices/Approximating_Maximum_Clients_per_Access_Point and https://webresources.ruckuswireless.com/datasheets/r550/ds-commscope-r550.html and https://support.hostifi.com/en/articles/6441143-unifi-wireless-best-practices
- [HIGH] **Beacon/management airtime is controllable to low single digits: at a 6 Mbps basic rate, 2 SSIDs on one AP measured ~1.1% channel utilization and 10 SSIDs ~5.6-8%, but at a 1 Mbps basic rate 11 SSIDs project to ~70% — so run ONE SSID, 20MHz, and raise the minimum basic rate after bench-validating the bulbs**
  - wifi-professionals.com validated the Revolution Wi-Fi SSID Overhead Calculator against live capture: 2 SSIDs/1 AP @6Mbps = 1.12% predicted vs ~1% measured; 10 SSIDs = 5.62% predicted vs ~8% measured; 14 SSIDs/2 co-channel APs = ~11%; legacy 1 Mbps beacon rate with 11 SSIDs on a shared channel ≈ 70% utilization before any data flows. Design rule for the canopy VLAN: exactly 1 SSID on the 2.4GHz radios, disable 802.11b rates (set minimum/basic rate to 6, 11 or 12 Mbps — McCann notes 'disabling rates below 6 or 11 Mbps can improve efficiency of higher-density networks' but must be compatibility-tested against the bulbs), beacon interval left at 100ms, and disable extra SSIDs on those radios (each UniFi AP supports 4-8 SSIDs/band — use as few as possible). Keep guest/kiosk/staff SSIDs on 5GHz radios only.
  - src: https://www.wifi-professionals.com/2018/08/ssids-overhead-effect-on-channel-utilisation and https://revolutionwifi.blogspot.com/2013/10/ssid-overhead-how-many-wi-fi-ssids-are.html and https://evanmccann.net/blog/2021/11/unifi-advanced-wi-fi-settings
- [HIGH] **UniFi-specific IoT settings consensus: 20MHz width on 2.4GHz, channels 1/6/11 only, LOWERED transmit power (high TX power hurts in dense co-channel rooms), band steering off for IoT SSIDs, multicast/broadcast control enabled, and optionally pin groups of bulbs to specific APs via AP groups**
  - HostiFi's UniFi IoT guide: use non-overlapping 1/6/11, 'lower transmit power on 2.4GHz access points', create IoT SSIDs scoped to single APs via AP groups because cheap IoT clients 'were never designed to deal with one SSID being broadcast multiple times from different APs' (they associate to a random/weak AP and never roam). For the static canopy this AP-pinning trick maps perfectly onto the 5-zone structure: e.g. 5 SSID-to-AP-group mappings (one per zone of 40 bulbs, matching the 5 separate accounts) guarantees even client distribution instead of 120 bulbs dogpiling one AP — at the cost of 5 SSIDs total, still fine at 6-12Mbps beacon rates if each radio carries only its own zone's SSID. McCann adds: enable Multicast and Broadcast Control (filters chatty broadcast) for high-density, leave DTIM auto, WPA2 + PMF off.
  - src: https://support.hostifi.com/en/articles/7019454-unifi-recommend-settings-for-iot-devices and https://evanmccann.net/blog/2021/11/unifi-advanced-wi-fi-settings
- [HIGH] **DHCP is trivial at this scale if sized deliberately: use a /23 or larger flat scope (510+ hosts) on the IoT VLAN, alert at 80% utilization, and plan for the reconnect burst — the risky moment is 200 bulbs DHCP-ing and re-establishing TLS simultaneously after a power cut, which the 5-zone circuit design should stagger**
  - High-density event guidance: /24 (254 hosts) is the classic failure ('can exceed the limit within minutes'), convert dense VLANs to /22 (1022) or /21 (2046); keep pools under 80% at peak; short 1-4h leases suit churny guest networks but for ~230 static devices a 12-24h lease with a /23 avoids both exhaustion and renewal chatter. Reserve static DHCP mappings for kiosk, bridge, APs, and vignette iPads. Operationally: bring canopy zones up one 40-bulb circuit at a time (30-60s apart) at doors-open to avoid a 200-client association+DHCP+TLS thundering herd on the APs and the Afero cloud connection.
  - src: https://www.purple.ai/en-gb/guides/causes-dhcp-timeouts-high-density-wifi and https://itman.ae/2026/09/14/high-density-wifi-how-to-support/
- [MEDIUM] **Bulbs permanently store the provisioned SSID+password and there is no remote credential update: changing the network name or PSK after provisioning orphans every bulb until each is individually re-onboarded — freeze SSID/PSK before the first of 200 bulbs is claimed**
  - Hubspace support traffic confirms devices lose connection permanently when the router/SSID/password changes ('the transformer is still linked to your old WiFi... stored network credentials no longer match') and the fix is per-device re-setup through the app. For the activation: finalize network naming, PSK, and security mode in the shop BEFORE provisioning begins, provision against the exact production SSID (bring the actual event APs to the provisioning bench), and never touch credentials after install. A same-SSID/same-PSK spare AP is also the correct hot-spare strategy — bulbs will rejoin any AP broadcasting the identical WPA2 SSID.
  - src: https://www.asktech.support/electronics/tgori-hubspace-unable-transformer-phone-app.html and https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/c2/c289878d-e597-4484-82a4-0819d7060c0c.pdf
- [HIGH] **Cellular WAN redundancy: Peplink MAX BR1 Pro 5G (~$700-900 street, one listing $1,233.99) provides hot-failover/WAN-smoothing between wired venue internet and 5G; the cheaper pattern is a Balance 20X (~$500) with cellular FlexModule or a 5G business-internet gateway as secondary WAN**
  - Since ALL control (kiosk bridge -> Afero cloud -> bulbs) and even the Hubspace app die without internet, dual-WAN is mandatory for the show. Peplink MAX BR1 Pro 5G: 5G/LTE Cat-20, Wi-Fi 6, hot failover + WAN smoothing, InControl2 cloud management, proven in event/mobile deployments; Balance 20X supports xDSL primary + up to two cellular links via FlexModule Mini. Cradlepoint (E-series + NetCloud) is the enterprise alternative with strong IoT protocol support. Note two latency caveats for acceptance targets: cellular adds ~30-80ms (irrelevant vs the <2s scene-start target), and carrier CGNAT is fine because all Afero connections are outbound TLS. Order SIMs early and verify 5G band coverage inside the Charleston venue during the site survey; a dual-SIM (Verizon+T-Mobile) config covers carrier-level outages.
  - src: https://www.amazon.com/Peplink-Solution-Wi-Fi-MU-MIMO-MAX-BR1-PRO-5GH-T-PRM/dp/B09K7SRMX4 and https://forum.peplink.com/t/presales-max-br1-pro-vs-balance-20x-for-network-backup/40936 and https://5gstore.com/blog/2026/07/19/cradlepoint-router-guide/
- [MEDIUM] **No public per-account device cap exists for Hubspace/Afero, and no documented real-world installation approaches 200 devices on one account — the largest visible community installs are a few dozen devices, making the 200-bulb single-account (or even 5x40) configuration genuinely unproven territory**
  - Neither the Home Depot Hubspace FAQ, the app-store listings, the A19 manual, nor the HA integration repos state any device limit per account; GitHub issue mining on jdeath/Hubspace-Homeassistant surfaced no 'I have 100+ devices' reports; HA community threads describe the integration as workable but 'updates are slow and the UI can be unresponsive' even at hobbyist scale. Home Depot's own advice to the project (separate accounts per vignette) is consistent with known cloud-side throttling rather than an association limit. The 5-accounts-for-5-zones split (40 bulbs each) both shards any per-account rate limit and matches the aioafero one-account-per-bridge-instance model — but the Sep 23 bench gate should include the largest single-account bulb count you can assemble.
  - src: https://community.home-assistant.io/t/hubspace-integration-question/960804 and https://github.com/jdeath/Hubspace-Homeassistant and https://www.homedepot.com/c/ab/hubspace-faq/9ba683603be9fa5395fab9014e4790c0
- [MEDIUM] **Provisioning labor estimate: at the app's one-at-a-time cadence (~2-5 min per bulb including QR scan, BLE handshake, Wi-Fi join, cloud registration, naming, and room/group assignment), 200 bulbs cost roughly 7-17 person-hours — parallelize with one phone per account/zone since a single account's app session is the serialization point**
  - Home Depot marketing claims setup 'in less than 5 minutes' per product and reviewers corroborate a few minutes per device when nothing goes wrong; failures (QR won't scan -> toggle Bluetooth, retry; wrong network cached) add retries. Five zones x five phones x 40 bulbs at ~3 min average is about 2 hours of parallel wall-clock work — schedule provisioning as a dedicated pre-install bench day (label each bulb with zone+grid position while its QR card is in hand), NOT during the Oct 20-23 install window. Unknown and bench-critical: whether Afero's cloud rate-limits rapid sequential claims on one account (e.g., bulb #35 starts failing), and whether two phones on the SAME account can claim concurrently.
  - src: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=io.afero.partner.hubspace&hl=en_US and https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/c2/c289878d-e597-4484-82a4-0819d7060c0c.pdf
- [MEDIUM] **Wi-Fi 7 / newest-gen APs buy nothing for this project: 2.4GHz-only b/g/n clients get zero benefit from U7/6E/7 features, and 6GHz-capable APs sometimes complicate 2.4GHz IoT onboarding (WPA3 requirements on merged SSIDs)**
  - The bulbs are legacy 20MHz b/g/n clients; per-AP 2.4GHz radios remain 2x2 or 4x4 across U6/U7/Ruckus generations, so capacity per radio is essentially unchanged — spend budget on MORE mid-tier APs and clean channel reuse rather than flagship models. 6GHz-inclusive SSIDs mandate WPA3-SAE which, when carelessly merged with 2.4GHz on one SSID name, silently enables transition mode and breaks IoT joins — a recurring failure mode in mixed fleets. A dedicated 2.4GHz-only WPA2 SSID sidesteps the whole class of problems.
  - src: https://www.geeksinsneaks.com/resources/fixes/mixing-wpa3-wpa2-breaks-older-devices and https://help.ui.com/hc/en-us/articles/32065480092951-UniFi-WiFi-SSID-and-AP-Settings-Overview

## Bench tests
- Association-density knee test: join 60-70 bulbs to ONE AP's 2.4GHz radio (production AP model, 20MHz, WPA2-only, channel 6), then run 100 scene changes; record channel utilization, join stability, and scene-apply latency distribution to find the practical per-radio ceiling before finalizing AP count (target: confirm 40-50/radio is comfortable).
- Minimum-basic-rate compatibility: on the test SSID, disable 802.11b rates (min/basic = 12 Mbps), power-cycle 10 bulbs and confirm they rejoin and pass traffic; repeat at 6 Mbps and at default-all-rates. If bulbs fail at 12, fall back to 6; if they fail at 6, document that 1-2 Mbps beacons are required and re-run the density test.
- Security-mode join matrix: 5 bulbs x {WPA2-PSK/PMF-off, WPA2-PSK/PMF-optional, WPA2/WPA3-transition, WPA3-only} — record join success and time-to-join for each; expected result is WPA2/PMF-off works and WPA3-only fails, validating the network spec.
- Provisioning-at-scale drill: time 10 consecutive QR onboardings on one account/one phone (capture per-bulb minutes and any cloud errors after N rapid claims); then attempt simultaneous claiming from TWO phones logged into the SAME account; then claim bulb #11 from a different account by scanning its QR to verify the documented cross-account transfer works while the bulb is online, and note whether Wi-Fi credentials survive the transfer.
- Power-cut recovery storm: with 40 provisioned bulbs on one AP, kill mains for 10 minutes, restore all at once, and measure time until 100% report online/controllable (DHCP burst + TLS reconnect); repeat restoring in two 20-bulb waves 30s apart to quantify the benefit of staggered zone power-up against the <60s recovery acceptance target.
- Credential-freeze validation: change ONLY the Wi-Fi password on the test AP and confirm 3 bulbs become permanently unreachable (no remote credential update) and require full app re-onboarding — proves the 'lock SSID/PSK before provisioning' rule to the whole team.
- Factory-reset procedure discovery: on 3 production A19 bulbs, test the community reset (rapid on/off x3, x5, and 10s-on/2s-cycles variants) and document the exact sequence and blink signature that returns a bulb to pairing mode, since the official manual omits it.
- 5G failover cutover: run the kiosk bridge through a Peplink (or equivalent) with venue WAN primary + 5G secondary; yank the primary WAN mid-scene-change and measure (a) time until Afero commands succeed again, (b) whether Conclave push reconnects automatically, and (c) scene-apply latency over cellular vs wired — validates the <10s worst-case scene target under failover.
- BLE + Wi-Fi coexistence floor: with all ~200 bulbs powered in the shop, capture a 2.4GHz spectrum scan (e.g., WiFiman/Chanalyzer) and confirm BLE advertising from the fleet does not raise noise floor materially on channels 1/6/11; simultaneously verify the Hubspace app can still BLE-control one bulb from 10 ft away through the RF crowd.
- AP-pinning distribution test: configure 5 SSIDs (one per zone) each broadcast by only its zone's AP via AP groups, verify each provisioned zone's 40 bulbs associate only to their AP and the client-count spread stays 40/40/40/40/40 after a full power cycle (guards against the sticky-random-association failure mode of one shared SSID).

- Is there any hard or soft per-account device cap on Hubspace/Afero accounts, and do rapid sequential claims (40+ bulbs in an hour) trip cloud-side anti-abuse throttling? No public documentation; only the Sep 23 bench can answer.
- What are the actual numeric Afero cloud API rate limits (requests/min per account, per token, per IP) and does the kiosk's 5-zone fan-out (5 accounts from one venue IP) share any per-IP bucket? Community code only reveals a 2s practical floor.
- Does the QR-based cross-account transfer preserve the bulb's stored Wi-Fi credentials (pure cloud re-claim) or force a full BLE re-provision — and does it work while the bulb is online on the old account?
- Exact Wi-Fi silicon in the current-production A19 RGBW bulb (ESP32-class Leedarson module like the dimmer's LA02301, or Realtek): fccid.io/fcc.report block automated retrieval of the 2AB2Q-12A800STQT internal photos — pull them manually or tear down one bulb, since chipset determines min-rate and PMF tolerance.
- Do Hubspace bulbs tolerate an 802.11b-disabled network (basic rate 6-12 Mbps)? No public reports either way; this single setting is worth ~3-10x beacon-airtime savings and must be bench-proven.
- Will 5 co-located 2.4GHz radios at minimum TX power in a 34x37 ft room (two channel pairs reused) stay below ~50% channel utilization during a full 200-bulb scene cascade?
