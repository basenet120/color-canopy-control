# Synthesis — canopy control model (2026-09-26)

## Verdict
Yes, with one real caveat. The 9-kit canopy is a small, manageable cloud control problem: 9 devices, one PUT per module per mode change (about 22x fewer calls than the 200-bulb design), and all animation runs on the controllers with no ongoing cloud traffic. Whole-strand white, colour and preset looks (Lowcountry Calm, Goodnight Glow, Holiday at Home) are supported by aioafero today. The per-head painted looks (Rainbow Row Sunset, Garden Gathering, the Golden Hour band) rest on an inference from sibling Hubspace strings made by other manufacturers: a 30-entry color-sequence-v2 slot that we can write and select in one call. If that fails, they fall back cleanly to slots hand-painted in the app and selected by API, then to one colour per module, with the kiosk copy adjusted to match. Golden Hour's 'moving across the canopy' is the weakest link, because nothing synchronises the 9 controllers. It needs either slow on-device motion with the same wire direction in every module (a routing request to VWBD that must land before fabrication) or a stepped sweep, plus client sign-off from bench video. The single biggest unknown is the SQHDK03's actual cloud profile and whether it accepts third-party frame and slot writes. Buy one $99 kit now and run the 1-hour dump and replay test rather than waiting for VWBD's Prototype 01.

## Control model
Treat the canopy as nine independent Afero metadevices on one dedicated canopy Hubspace account. Each module is one SQHDK03 controller driving 30 addressable pucks (RGB plus cool and warm white), 270 heads in total. The Conductor holds the account and runs on the venue mini PC; the four vignettes stay on their own accounts. The usable API has no group, scene, multi-device or sync call, so every mode compiles to exactly one PUT per module to /v1/accounts/{aid}/metadevices/{id}/state (Host: semantics2.afero.net). That PUT carries the module's full state vector: power, color-mode, look selector, brightness, speed, party-mode off, and lastUpdateTime in epoch ms. A mode change is therefore 9 PUTs, fired as one concurrent burst over a pre-warmed session (limit_per_host of at least 9; aioafero's default is 3) with a token refreshed ahead of time (access tokens live about 118 s). Each change is then confirmed by Conclave echo or REST readback, and any module that differs gets its state re-sent. There is no per-head endpoint. A head is an index into the framebuffer of a color-sequence-v2 object stored in one of three custom slots per device (custom-1..3, attributes 351-353), and a slot is shown with color-individual=custom-N (attr 12) plus color-mode=individual (attr 5). That model assumes the Nanoleaf-built SQHDK03 exposes the same profile as its Novolink, EcoSmart and Leedarson siblings, which stays unverified until a kit is dumped. The Conductor owns the canvas: it composes 270-head looks in canopy coordinates and splits them into nine 30-entry frames using a per-module index->(x,y) map (wire routing plus A/B orientation, confirmed by calibration). During the show, the three painted modes (Rainbow Row Sunset, Garden Gathering, Golden Hour) stay stored in the three slots and are only selected, with a PUT under 1 KB. Full frames of about 3.4 KB are written only at commissioning, when the reconciler finds a slot has drifted, or if more than three painted looks are ever needed. Whole-strand white/CCT, single colour and firmware presets with speed are first-class aioafero calls (Calm, Goodnight Glow, Holiday) and need no per-head support. All animation runs on each controller after one write, on its own free-running clock. Motion across modules therefore has two options: slow on-device motion, made to look continuous by consistent wire direction plus a tight start burst, or a sweep stepped by the Conductor at no faster than one step every 2 s. Every mode carries a fallback ladder. T1: frames written by the API. T2: slots hand-painted in the app and selected by the API. T3: whole-strand and preset looks, including composition at module level, where each module acts as one coarse pixel.

## Device math
Canopy: 9 kits x 30 pucks = 270 heads = 9 Afero metadevices on one canopy account, against 200 metadevices in the A19 design (about 22x fewer). The vignettes add roughly 15-25 devices on 4 separate accounts, so 5 accounts in total.

Per mode change: 9 PUTs, one per module. A slot-select, white or preset look is under 1 KB. A full 30-entry frame is about 3.4 KB. Aioafero 9.3.0 emits one request for frame + select + mode + power: the offline harness was re-run on Sep 26 against the 20-segment string dump; cloud acceptance is unverified. Add 9 readback GETs and up to 9 re-assert PUTs, for at most 27 requests per change. At one guest change every 10 s that is about 0.1 writes/s per device, against a budget of 1 write/s per device.

Stepped Golden Hour: 3-9 PUTs per step, i.e. about 3-4.5 PUT/s at 2-3 s steps, for as long as it runs.

Slots: 3 per module x 9 = 27 stored patterns. That is exactly enough for 3 painted modes (Sunset, Garden, Golden Hour). Calm and Goodnight Glow use whole-strand white; Holiday uses a firmware preset. Hand-paint fallback: 3 x 9 x 30 = 810 puck assignments, about 4-7 staff-hours.

Chaining option: 60 pucks per controller gives 5 controllers (4x60 + 1x30); frame length unknown. Frame size (inference): about 7 bytes per entry, so 30 entries plus header is about 220 bytes, under a 255-byte non-Potenco attribute cap; 60 entries (about 430 bytes) would not fit.

Electrical: 9 x 36 W = 324 W maximum, about 2.7 A at 120 V, through 9 power supplies rated indoor/dry only.

Cable: 29 gaps x 19.7 in = about 571 in of puck-to-puck cable. The head path per module is about 294-300 in (gaps of 10-11.2 in on the 8/7/8/7 lattice). That leaves about 270-280 in (about 22-23 ft) of fixed slack per module, 8.5-9.7 in per gap, to be looped above the panel and never cut.

Tokens: access tokens last about 118 s, so refresh before every burst.

Spares: at least 2 kits (controller and power supply are not sold separately; warranty is void in commercial use) plus 2 SQHDE03-5PSL strings. 12 kits x $99 Special Buy (through Nov 3) = about $1,188 plus tax.

## Modes
### LOWCOUNTRY CALM (warm ivory + relaxed lamp glow)
- technique: Whole-strand tunable white on all 9 modules in one burst: color-mode=white, color-temperature about 2700 K (bench 2700 vs 3000 K through frosted Mylar), brightness about 55-65%, power on, party-mode off. If the SQHDK profile lists the attr-300 mood programs, 'dinner-party' (2800 K 62%) or 'chill' (3200 K 40%) can replace this so the signage can name an app mood. A painted variant with 2400-3000 K 'lamp-glow' pools needs per-puck white to be proven and a fourth painted look (write-then-select), so it is optional only.
- Hubspace feature demonstrated: Tunable white (2200-6500 K hardware) and dimming, both standard Hubspace app controls; conditionally, a named app mood program.
- calls per change: 9 PUTs (one per module, under 1 KB, 4-5 values); nothing ongoing; plus 9 readback GETs.
- risk: Low. Whole-strand CCT and brightness on SQHDK are assumed from sibling profiles; the dump confirms. The API CCT range may start at 2700 K (the Hampton Bay string profile exposes 2700-6500 even though hardware can go lower). aioafero set_state drops fields that match its cache, so send the full vector via update(states=...).
- fallback: RGB-mixed warm ivory (for example 255,190,120) at the same brightness. Manual fallback: a Hubspace app Group of the 9 modules sets CCT and brightness from a staff phone.
### RAINBOW ROW SUNSET (orange, peach + warm white)
- technique: Per-head canvas look: a sunset gradient composed across the full 225x120 in canopy (Home Depot orange, starting from #F96302, then peach, then warm white, in bands whose direction is set in config), split into nine 30-entry frames through each module's index map. It is stored once in custom-1 of every module at commissioning (one full-frame PUT per module) and selected during the show with color-individual=custom-1 + color-mode=individual + power. Colour recipe comes from the bench: orange and peach probably need RGB blended with the warm-white LEDs (whiteBrightness above 0 per entry), if the device honours per-puck white.
- Hubspace feature demonstrated: Individual puck 'paint' and saved custom patterns (3 per device), the kit's headline Hubspace app feature.
- calls per change: During the show: 9 small select PUTs. Commissioning or repair: 9 full-frame PUTs (about 3.4 KB each, one per module). Nothing ongoing.
- risk: Medium-high. Unverified: color-sequence-v2 with 30 entries on SQHDK, and whether the cloud accepts third-party frame writes. One reviewer reports orange and peach render weakly on the single RGB die. Per-puck warm white may be ignored. The index map depends on VWBD's routing and orientation.
- fallback: T2: staff hand-paint the per-module pattern into custom-1 in the Hubspace app (about 15-20 min per module) and the kiosk only selects it. T3: a sunset at module level, one whole-strand colour per module row (top row Home Depot orange, middle peach, bottom warm white), still 9 PUTs; the copy changes to 'each strand set to its own colour'.
### GARDEN GATHERING (warm pathway + canopy glow)
- technique: Per-head look in custom-2: a brighter warm 'pathway' (2-3 heads wide, meandering along the 225 in length, about 2700 K at high brightness) over a dim warm 'canopy glow' base (about 2200 K or amber at 20-30%). It is composed at canvas level, stored at commissioning and selected during the show. Once effect codes are known, a very slow on-device brightness effect (a breathing glow) can optionally be added to the slot header.
- Hubspace feature demonstrated: Per-puck paint with per-puck brightness (and per-puck white temperature, if supported) in a saved custom pattern; optionally an on-device brightness effect.
- calls per change: During the show: 9 small select PUTs. Commissioning: 9 full-frame PUTs. Nothing ongoing.
- risk: Medium-high. Same profile and write unknowns as Sunset. Per-puck white temperature is unverified (the sibling RGB profiles carry 'no-white'); without it, 'warm white' is RGB-mixed and reads orange or pink. How legible the pathway is through Mylar is untested.
- fallback: T2: a hand-painted slot. T3 at module level: centre-row (or diagonal) modules at 2700 K 100% as the 'pathway', the rest at 2200 K about 25%, 9 PUTs.
### HOLIDAY AT HOME (warm living-room + holiday lighting)
- technique: Firmware holiday preset on all 9 modules: color-mode=sequence + color-sequence/preset=<name> + speed/color-sequence (slow, -6 to -10), plus brightness about 70%. Speed is re-sent every time because it resets after power-off on sibling strings. Pick a twinkle-type preset that looks fine without phase sync, from the SQHDK list (candidates from sibling profiles: snowfall, white-star, mistletoe, christmas), so the 9 unsynchronised controller clocks don't look broken. If every preset is too garish for 'warm living-room', use a painted warm-white base with sparse red and green accents instead (needs a slot or write-then-select).
- Hubspace feature demonstrated: Built-in holiday presets (app Presets > Winter Holidays) with effect speed, running on each strand's own controller.
- calls per change: 9 PUTs (under 1 KB) per change; nothing ongoing.
- risk: Medium-low. SQHDK preset names and looks are unknown until dumped. Chase-type presets expose the lack of sync between modules. Speed may not persist. A preset may override brightness.
- fallback: Another preset; a painted static holiday look; or, at module level, warm-white modules with red and green accent modules.
### GOLDEN HOUR (rich amber wash moving across the canopy)
- technique: Primary, pending the bench: an amber custom pattern in custom-3 whose header carries an on-device motion effect at the slowest usable speed. Each module's frame holds exactly one soft amber band (2200 K white blended with RGB amber) over deep amber, one band per module width, so in-phase scrolling reads as continuous amber waves crossing the canopy. This needs three things: head index rising with canvas X in every module (VWBD column-wise zigzag routing from each module's canvas-left edge, B modules included); a tight start burst of 9 PUTs; and a slow period (at least 20-30 s to cross a module), so start skew and drift stay a small fraction of the cycle. Re-trigger on every selection to re-align. Secondary: a sweep stepped by the Conductor, so a single wash crosses the whole canopy (per-module frames, or whole-strand brightness by module column, no faster than one step every 2 s, running for a bounded time and then holding).
- Hubspace feature demonstrated: A custom pattern with an animated motion effect and speed (effects in the app's pattern editor) running on each strand's own controller, with all nine started together by the kiosk. The stepped variant shows per-puck colour and brightness sequenced by the kiosk.
- calls per change: Primary: 9 PUTs at start (select only if stored; about 3.4 KB each if written), nothing ongoing. Stepped: 3-9 PUTs per step, about 3-4.5 PUT/s at 2-3 s steps, for as long as it runs.
- risk: High. motionEffect codes and behaviour are unknown and may not be a directional scroll. Nothing syncs the controllers: skew equals the cloud delivery spread, then clocks drift freely. B modules move the opposite way unless re-routed, and software cannot reverse on-device motion. Row-serpentine routing makes the motion zig-zag. The stepped sweep risks silent throttling and looks steppy (the acceptance targets allow a 2 s spread between modules). Speed resets after power loss.
- fallback: (1) A static canvas amber gradient with a slow on-device brightness effect; the copy says 'amber glow', not 'moving'. (2) A stepped brightness sweep at module level across the 3 module columns (small PUTs). (3) Two modules per controller (60 pucks) to widen the synced area, if the chaining bench shows 60 independent entries. (4) Last resort, under the client's supplemental-lighting rule: a non-Hubspace wash for the motion, with kiosk copy that never attributes it to Hubspace.
### THE GOODNIGHT GLOW (soft warm dimmed light)
- technique: Whole-strand warm white at the lowest supported CCT (2200 K if the API allows it, otherwise 2700 K) and about 8-12% brightness on all 9 modules. Optionally, the Conductor steps the dim down from the previous look (3 steps over about 10 s, within 1 write/s per device) so the 'goodnight' moment is visible. If the profile exposes attr-300 'nightlight' (2700 K 5%) or 'sleep' (40% to 5% over 30 min, on-device), use it and name it in the copy, though the 30-min Sleep fade is too slow to notice during a kiosk visit.
- Hubspace feature demonstrated: Dimming plus warm tunable white; conditionally, the app's Nightlight or Sleep program running on-device.
- calls per change: 9 PUTs (plus 18 if the 3-step dim is used); nothing ongoing.
- risk: Low. Low-end dimming may step or flicker at 1-10%. The API CCT floor may be 2700 K. Guests will not perceive a 30-min fade.
- fallback: RGB deep amber at low brightness, or 2700 K at 10%.

## Prototype 01 bench plan
1. 0 - Don't wait for VWBD. Buy one kit now for the control bench (three for steps 16-18; they later become show units or spares) and run steps 1-13 on a table with Wi-Fi. 2. 1 - Label and hardware audit (15 min): photograph the controller label (FCC ID, model, firmware if printed) and the power-supply markings (input/output, Class 2, IP rating). Count parts against the manual. Caliper the puck (with and without ears, screw spacing) and the screw-lock connector the panel must clear. Measure the power-supply-to-controller lead, the controller output lead, the 9.8 ft extension, and each string's lead to its first puck.
3. 2 - Onboard the kit to a dedicated bench Hubspace account (MFA off) on a 2.4 GHz SSID set up like the show network, and time it. Confirm the app shows ONE device with 30 paintable pucks. Mint a refresh token on the laptop (email OTP) and store it outside git.
4. 3 - GATE A, read-only profile dump. Run aioafero fetch_discovery_data/fetch_metadevice (expansions=state,capabilities,semantics), anonymize the output and commit it as the SimDriver fixture. Record: model, manufacturer and firmware; color-individual names and hints ('30-segment'? '60-segment'? 'no-white'?); color-sequence-v2 instances and framebuffer length; color-mode values (is 'individual' present?); color-temperature min and max (2200 or 2700?); preset names and category hints; attr-300 programs (sleep, nightlight, dinner-party); speed range; party-mode; restore-values. Pass = 3 slots with a 30-entry (or fixed 60-entry) frame, so the per-head path is alive; fail = T3 only. Optional read-only raw capture: GET .../devices/{deviceId}?expansions=attributes to pair the attribute-351 hex with its decoded JSON.
5. 4 - App-paint readback and effect table, with no writes from us. In the app, paint an index-coded pattern into slot 1 (puck 1 red, 2 green, 3 blue, 4 white, the rest dim). GET it and confirm the entry order, and that index 0 is the puck nearest the controller. Then, in the app's pattern editor, apply each effect and several speeds, GET after each change, and tabulate motionEffect, brightnessEffect, motionSpeed, brightnessSpeed and brightnessDepth.
6. 5 - GATE B, slot select: PUT color-individual=custom-2 + color-mode=individual + power (the PR #61 approach). Pass = visible switch within about 2 s and a matching readback. That proves store-and-select (T2) works even if frame writes don't.
7. 6 - GATE C, replay write: PUT the exact color-sequence-v2 object read from slot 1 into slot 2, plus select. Pass = HTTP 200 (not 400), GET returns it unchanged, and the lights match; that proves frames written by the API (T1). On failure, stop at T2 (hand-painted slots) and only then study the raw attribute path.
8. 7 - Synthetic frame: give each index a unique hue (hue = 12 deg x i) and photograph it for the index map. Film against a millisecond clock to confirm all 30 pucks change in the same video frame. Run 20 trials for PUT-to-light latency (median and p95).
9. 8 - Edge cases: 29-, 31- and 60-entry frames (HTTP status, GET-back length, what the lights show). Per-puck white (cct 2200/2700, whiteBrightness 100, colorBrightness 0) and RGB plus white mixed in one entry. Rewriting the active slot without reselecting it. Order of values within the PUT. Whether frames cut or fade.
10. 9 - Whole-strand and preset modes: CCT floor and ceiling via the API; dimming smoothness at 1-10%; color-rgb Home Depot orange; every preset at speed -10, 0 and +10; attr-300 programs if listed. Confirm each is one PUT through standard aioafero calls. Shortlist Holiday presets that look fine without sync.
11. 10 - On-device proof: with a preset and a slot-with-motion running, cut the router's WAN for 10-30 min. The animation must continue. Time the reconnect and check the state afterwards.
12. 11 - Motion enumeration via the API: sweep the motionEffect and brightnessEffect codes across speeds, filming each. Find a directional scroll, its direction relative to the index, and its slowest period. Test whether re-sending the select restarts the phase (the re-alignment lever for Golden Hour).
13. 12 - Power and detection: pull mains power in each mode (white, slot, preset, party) under each restore setting, and record what comes back, including speed. 10 cold boots must always report 30 heads (the 12-puck failure). Reseat one connector and repeat.
14. 13 - Single-device rate: 50 frame writes at 5 s, 2 s and 1 s spacing. Log HTTP codes and compare each GET-back with what was sent to catch silent drops. Stop at the first 429 or 403.
15. 14 - On Prototype 01 with Mylar, colour study at show brightness: RGB-only vs 2200 K vs RGB plus warm-white blend, for amber, Home Depot orange, peach and warm ivory, through clear and frosted Mylar at 6-24 in. Take photos at equal exposure and lux readings, pick recipes, and get client eyes on them.
16. 15 - On Prototype 01: install the final routing (no cuts, slack looped above the panel). Run 4 h at 100% cool white while logging controller, power-supply, cable and puck temperatures (the manual forbids enclosed spaces above 40 C). Repeat the cold-boot detection check. Confirm how the concealed controller buttons and microphone behave. Run the hue-ramp calibration against the real routing in both A and B orientation.
17. 16 - Start skew with 2-3 kits: fire the same preset and the same slot-with-motion as parallel PUTs (limit_per_host at least 9, warmed session, fresh token), and also through an app Group. Film at 120 fps or more, over 20 trials, and measure the first-to-last start spread.
18. 17 - Drift: run the chosen Golden Hour motion for 10, 30 and 60 min, measure phase drift between controllers, and test whether a re-trigger re-aligns them. Decide primary vs stepped Golden Hour and send the footage for client sign-off.
19. 18 - Chaining: put two kits' strings on one controller (60 pucks). Check detection, framebuffer length (60 independent entries or 30 repeated), and motion continuity across both halves. This decides whether 2-module controllers are a Golden Hour option.
20. 19 - Nine kits before install: onboard all 9 (plus spares) to the canopy account. Run 50 bursts of 9 parallel select and full-frame PUTs, and 30 min of stepped sweep at 2-3 s per step, with concurrent vignette-like traffic on the other accounts. Measure the acceptance targets (visible start within 2 s at p95, spread within 2 s at p95, self-heal within 10 s) and count silent drops.
21. 20 - Failure drills: pull a string mid-show; swap a controller or kit (onboard the spare, remap it in config, recommission its slots, recalibrate); and cut the WAN (the current look must be re-applied within 60 s of its return).

## Build implications
1. Config and device registry: each module maps to {account, metadevice_id, canvas origin, orientation A/B, a 30-entry index->(x,y) map, slot assignment}. It must be editable from the ops UI, because a swapped controller gets a new metadevice id. The orientation scheme (deck p.14 A/B/A vs p.18 AAA/BBB/AAA) lives in config, never in code.
2. Capability detection driven by the device profile, at startup and after every power event: read each module's functions and state; derive the segment count (the color-individual hint, or the framebuffer length), slot names, color-mode values, CCT min/max, preset names, speed range, party-mode and restore-values. The sibling string exposes 2700-6500 K via the API even though its hardware goes lower. Refuse per-head writes if the count is not 30 and flag the module 'Needs attention'; this doubles as the 12-puck length-detection detector.
3. Afero adapter write path: aioafero 9.3.0 has no frame or slot-select API and ignores color-individual/color-sequence-v2 rows; PR #61 is stale, so assume it never lands. Build states by hand and send them with bridge.lights.update(id, states=[...]), or with a raw PUT through the adapter's own session if aioafero does not classify SQHDK03 as a Light. Always use epoch-ms lastUpdateTime: seconds get HTTP 200 and are silently dropped (aioafero #65). Always send the full state vector, because set_state drops fields that match its cache.
4. Frame writes copy the header (id, version, headerFlags, sequenceFlags, frameBuffer.flags) and the framebuffer length from the device's own readback. Only colour entries and bench-verified effect fields change. The effect-code table lives in config.
5. Mode compiler with a per-mode fallback ladder in config: T1 frame write + select; T2 select a stored slot; T3 whole-strand CCT/RGB/preset; module-level colours; stepped choreography. The ladder step is chosen from detected capabilities and bench flags, so downgrading a mode is a config change, not a code change.
6. Slot manager: custom-1..3 are assigned to Sunset, Garden and Golden Hour. Commissioning writes the content (T1) or verifies the hand-painted content (T2), and stores a content hash. During the show, slots are only selected. The reconciler verifies slot contents by REST GET, because in aioafero 9.3.0 Conclave delivers attribute-351 changes as raw undecoded values (researcher inference). On drift it rewrites (T1) or alerts (T2), which also undoes staff edits made in the app.
7. Canvas frame composer: looks are designed in canopy coordinates (225x120 in, 270 heads) and split through the index maps. B-module mirroring is handled by the map, not by special cases. Entries are validated: r,g,b 0-255; cct within the device range; colorBrightness and whiteBrightness 0-100; exact length. Colour recipes (RGB-only vs white blend) come per mode from the bench colour study.
8. Burst executor, for sync: one aiohttp session per account with limit_per_host of at least 9 and warmed keep-alive, the token refreshed before expiry, bodies pre-built, and all 9 module PUTs sent concurrently. Log send, ack and echo times per module, and report first-to-last skew for every change (the target is 2 s or less at p95).
9. Rate budget: at most 1 write/s per device, bounded concurrency per account, latest-wins coalescing per device, and a minimum dwell/debounce on the kiosk. When idle, hold a look rather than cycling quickly through an attract loop. Stepped Golden Hour runs at no faster than one step every 2 s, with a hard total PUT/s cap and a bounded duration. It downgrades automatically to the static look on any burst of 429/403/5xx, or on a detected silent drop (readback differs from what was sent).
10. Reconciler and power events: re-send the full desired state 3-5 s after each change, and whenever Conclave reports a module back online. Always re-send speed (it resets after power-off on sibling strings) and party-mode=off. Set restore-values to 'restored' if the profile exposes it, but never rely on it.
11. Per-module health model: online; head count = 30; mode and slot match desired; slot hashes; age of the last echo. The ops dashboard shows Ready / Updating / Needs attention together with the physical fix (for example 'power-cycle module 5'). If VWBD provides switched power per module, expose a per-module power-cycle action; a LAN-controlled PDU is preferable to cloud smart plugs (general engineering).
12. SimDriver fidelity: seed the SQHDK profile from the real dump; until then, use the Novolink/RGBIC sibling dumps with length 30 as a provisional fixture. Model: async acks; echo latency; raw-value Conclave for attr 351; silent drop on seconds timestamps; configurable wrong-length behaviour; per-controller animation clocks with start skew and drift; speed reset on power loss; the 12-puck fault. The canopy visualizer approximates on-device effects so looks can be designed before hardware arrives.
13. Calibration tool: hue-code or blink index i on module m, and a tech taps its position on the visualizer. The index map is saved, and calibration is rerun after any module or controller swap.
14. Kiosk copy tied to the technique actually in use: each look carries its 'Hubspace feature' statement, and when a mode downgrades (for example Golden Hour to a static gradient), the copy changes automatically. The copy never implies that the Hubspace app itself paints 270 heads as one canvas, or that non-Hubspace lighting is Hubspace.
15. Safety gates: HUBSPACE_LIVE=1 plus a device allowlist per environment (bench kit vs show modules), so no cloud session, routine or test can write to show devices. Canopy code paths cannot touch vignette accounts. Use one refresh token per runtime: don't share a token between the laptop, the cloud bench and the venue box, since Keycloak rotation may invalidate the other copies (UNVERIFIED).
16. Bench tools as first-class CLI commands: dump, replay, hue-ramp index map, wrong-length, white channel, skew/drift timer, rate test, soak. All are gated by the live flag and write JSONL telemetry to reports/, so results feed the fallback ladder.
17. Generic N-segment support: the same slot and frame code should drive any Hubspace segmented string, for example the Glow Garden Hampton Bay strings with their '20-segment' profile. Key it off the device's hint rather than hard-coding 30.
18. Fallback write path documented but not built: the raw Afero attribute write (POST /v1/accounts/{aid}/devices/{deviceId}/actions with little-endian hex). Use it only if GATE C fails, only after learning attribute 351's byte layout from read-only ?expansions=attributes captures, and never on show hardware without a bench proof.
19. Inputs the software needs from VWBD before fabrication freezes. (1) Column-wise zigzag cable routing starting at each module's canvas-left edge, for all 9 modules, so the index rises with canvas X and on-device motion runs the same direction everywhere; gaps stay at or under 11.2 in, well inside the 19.7 in spacing. (2) Strings never cut; slack is looped above the panel, since cutting correlates with the 12-puck failure. (3) Controllers and power supplies hidden but reachable and ventilated: power supplies are indoor/dry only, and there must be no enclosed space above 40 C. (4) A label on each module with its canvas position and controller serial.
20. Operating rules written into the runbooks: no Hubspace app use on the canopy account during public hours; app versions frozen on staff devices; an app Group of the 9 modules kept as the manual fallback for whole-strand looks; spares onboarded ahead of time and held in config as unassigned spare devices.

## Contradictions
1. Per-zone clones. The original brief, the Sep 19 digest, docs/context/03-hubspace-platform-facts.md line 32 and the cloud researcher's draft CLAUDE.md all say aioafero splits multizone string lights into per-zone Light clones. The api-model researcher ran aioafero 9.3.0's light_callback on the Hampton Bay/Novolink dump and got only a '-light-party-mode' switch, with remove_original False; I re-ran the harness on Sep 26 with the same result. Stronger: api-model, because it is executed code. Per-zone clones apply to main/trim fixtures only.
2. Slot count. The brief says custom-1..custom-4. The dumps show custom-1..3 on the string light and the RGBIC tape, and custom-4 only on the Leedarson speaker flush mount; I verified that the color-individual function on the Novolink dump lists 3 slots with '20-segment' hints. Stronger: 3, which also matches PCWorld's 'three patterns' for the SQHDK.
3. Mode strategy. api-model: write the frame and select the slot on every mode change (idempotent, and it undoes app edits). Product: store 3 looks in the slots and just switch between them. Synthesis: store-and-select during the show; write only at commissioning, on repair, or if more than 3 painted looks are needed; the reconciler compares slot contents and rewrites on drift. Stronger: store-and-select, because it means smaller payloads and fewer controller flash rewrites, and it still works if third-party frame writes are rejected (slots hand-painted in the app).
4. Maximum pucks per controller. 60: the HD manual, the box, and a Dec 2025 Hubspace answer that calls 90 'early internal testing'. 90 pucks / 147 ft: older Nanoleaf-authored answers and Nanoleaf's own SKU. Stronger: 60, the official source for this SKU.
5. Sync across kits. Nanoleaf-authored HD Q&A answers (posted as 'Hubspace') promise syncing sets and matching animations; they describe the Nanoleaf app. The HD FAQ, Novolink and PCWorld say groups only send the same command to each member and separate runs cannot be programmed as one. Stronger: no sync on Hubspace.
6. Power-supply rating. The HD manual says indoor/dry only, not outdoors whatever the weather. PCWorld says IP65; the Nanoleaf SKU is IP67. Stronger: the HD manual for this SKU. The install is indoors, but the plenum's ventilation and heat still matter.
7. Semantic write path. api-model argues the /v1 semantic PUT will accept color-sequence-v2, because aioafero already writes other object formats with the same wrapper and the app edits slots remotely. app-behavior's decompile shows the app also calls raw /v1/.../devices/{id}/requests ('postDeviceRequests'), so pattern writes may bypass the semantic layer, which then may have no encoder for this format. Neither side has a captured successful write, so this is unresolved: GATE C decides. The schema inference is also weakened because it comes from Novolink, EcoSmart and Leedarson devices, while the SQHDK is Nanoleaf-built with HD-specific firmware.
8. Price. api-model: $119 from a search snippet. app-behavior: $99 Special Buy 09/23-11/03/2026, regular $119, from the live HD page; a tracker also showed $99 on Sep 24. Stronger: $99 now (two sources, one with a dated promo window).
9. Head shape. The VWBD deck and brief say 'octagonal'. The product researcher says hexagonal pucks with two screw ears, about 35x32 mm. The deck's product photo is ambiguous; measure a real puck before VWBD cuts panel openings. Not relevant to control.
10. Pitch vs cable (a fit conflict, not a source conflict). The design uses 10 in pitch; the cable spacing is fixed at 19.7 in. Official guidance on cutting contradicts itself: the manual shows splicing by a qualified professional, while a Hubspace answer says the strings are not designed to be cut. Cutting also correlates with the 12-puck detection failure. So loop the slack and don't cut.
11. Access to homedepot.com. The product researcher says HD blocks automated lookups and used Wayback. app-behavior cites live HD Q&A and review pages dated Sep 2026. The content agrees (Nanoleaf answering as 'Hubspace'), so this doesn't change any conclusion.
12. CCT floor. Product: 2200-6500 K hardware. The sibling HB string profile exposes color-temperature 2700-6500 through the API (verified in the dump). Goodnight Glow's 2200 K may be unreachable as a whole-strand CCT; check the SQHDK range.

Stronger: a refresh token in an environment variable, used by aioafero; a static bearer cannot work.
