# risk-legal

**HEADLINE:** There is zero public record of Afero/Home Depot ever blocking or C&D'ing the 4+year-old ecosystem of reverse-engineered Hubspace clients, but every unofficial route technically breaches a "personal, noncommercial use" license — so for a client-sanctioned commercial activation the cheap insurance is a written blessing routed through the existing THD relationship (Afero demonstrably sells commercial API access via negotiated Service Agreements), plus a zero-app-store distribution plan (PWA in Guided Access / ad-hoc signing) and nominative-fair-use discipline around the HUBSPACE mark.

## Findings
- [HIGH] **The Hubspace app's own Terms of Use license is expressly limited to 'personal, noncommercial use' — a commercial brand activation and a replacement client both fall outside the granted license**
  - The dedicated 'The Home Depot HubSpace App Terms of Use' PDF (doc #9541887, dated 6-4-2020, still served from homedepot.com) grants permission 'to view and use this App ... for your own personal, noncommercial use' and forbids modifying, distributing, or creating derivative works 'from this App'. Notably it contains NO explicit anti-bot, anti-automation, or anti-reverse-engineering clause and no unilateral account-termination clause; governing law is Georgia, venue Atlanta, and arbitration applies only 'WITH THE PRIOR AGREEMENT OF THE HOME DEPOT'. Copyright is held jointly by Home Depot Product Authority, LLC (HDPA) and Afero, Inc. Practical read: the activation is a commercial use, but the operative risk is contract/ToS breach (account termination), not any stronger hook — and the client (THD) is the counterparty who can waive it.
  - src: https://www.homedepot.com/hdus/en_US/DTCCOMNEW/fetch/THD-App-Terms-of-Use-6-4-2020-Final.pdf
- [MEDIUM] **The general homedepot.com Terms of Use (updated ~June 2026) adds the prohibitions the app ToU lacks: no bots/automated access and no reverse engineering/decompiling of the Site or Content**
  - Search-index text of homedepot.com/c/Terms_of_Use shows prohibitions on 'bots, crawlers, automated scripts, or other automated means' and on 'modify, adapt, translate, reverse engineer, decompile, disassemble, or create derivative works from the Site or any Content', with new 2026 AI-feature clauses. The page 403s automated fetches (Akamai), consistent with active anti-bot posture on the retail site. Whether these site terms attach to Afero-cloud API traffic from a bridge (api2.afero.net is Afero's domain, not 'the Site') is untested; the app ToU says the app terms + privacy statement 'constitute the entire agreement ... with respect to this App'. Treat as: THD claims broad anti-automation rights somewhere in the stack, so get written sanction rather than parsing which document controls.
  - src: https://www.homedepot.com/c/Terms_of_Use
- [HIGH] **Afero's Developer Terms of Service (the agreement you'd sign to get official access) bans reverse engineering, circumvention, commercialization, and even disclosing evaluation results — but ONLY binds parties who sign up; the community-library route never accepts it**
  - Afero Developer ToS (last updated 12/16/2016, still live on cdn.afero.io) §3(b): without prior written approval Developer will not '(i) modify or create derivative works ... (ii) work around, interfere with, exceed or circumvent any technical limitations ... (iii) reverse engineer, decompile ... (iv) publish, rent, lease ... or otherwise commercialize ... (v) release or disclose the results of Developer's use ... to any third party other than via the Site's developer forum ... (viii) use the Afero Platform in any way that ... accesses in an unauthorized manner the servers'. §2(a): any use beyond internal evaluation 'including selling or otherwise distributing any Integrated Device' requires 'a separate commercial service agreement signed by both parties (Service Agreement)' — this is the contractual proof that Afero sells commercial platform access. §3(d): Afero may impose 'maximum use and traffic limitations' and suspend at will. §13(i) No Publicity: no press release or public announcement about a relationship with Afero without written consent — directly constrains how a 'powered by Afero/Hubspace' pitch could be marketed if you ever sign this. Choice of law: California, Santa Clara County venue. Notices to legal@afero.io.
  - src: https://cdn.afero.io/tos/developer/v1/developer.html
- [HIGH] **Afero's API License Addendum reserves monitoring, instant revocation, and a 30-day data-retention cap — terms to expect in any blessed-access negotiation**
  - Addendum A to the Developer ToS: Afero 'may change, suspend or cancel any or all of the functionality of the Afero APIs ... at any time', may suspend immediately on 'actual or reasonably suspected security breach', requires developers to update within 30 days of API revisions, treats API keys as Afero Confidential Information, and §2(c) has Developer authorize Afero 'to monitor Developer's use of the Afero APIs, including by accessing Developer Software'. Developer Data may be retained max 30 days and must be destroyed on request. If the activation negotiates sanctioned access, these are the default paper terms; a short-term event rider (fixed term Oct 15-Nov 2, named account IDs, agreed request budget) is the realistic shape.
  - src: https://cdn.afero.io/tos/developer/v1/developer.html
- [HIGH] **Four-plus years of public, reverse-engineered Hubspace clients have operated with zero known C&Ds, DMCA takedowns, GitHub removals, or targeted blocking by Afero/Home Depot**
  - jdeath/Hubspace-Homeassistant has issues dating to July 2022 (issue #6) and is still actively maintained in Sept 2026; homebridge-hubspace (sajmonr) and a from-scratch 2025+ integration (Emkraan/homeassistant-hubspace) are public with 'unofficial, not affiliated' disclaimers; the aioafero PyPI package advertises 'Talk to the Afero API asynchronously'. Targeted searches for cease-and-desist/DMCA history return nothing, and the Hubitat community thread (Feb 2025) discusses reverse-engineering openly with no enforcement anxiety. Counterpoint discipline: absence of enforcement is not permission, tolerance of hobbyist HA users is not tolerance of a branded commercial kiosk, and the one documented disruption was an apparent THD-side backend migration that made devices vanish from the API (noted in the Emkraan README), i.e., breakage risk is operational, not legal.
  - src: https://github.com/jdeath/Hubspace-Homeassistant
- [MEDIUM] **Observed anti-abuse surface is mild: aggressive request rate-limiting (community floors polling at 2s), per-device 403s under some conditions, opt-in OTP/MFA, ~2-minute access tokens — but no reports of login lockouts, CAPTCHAs, or new-device blocks**
  - Emkraan README: 'State polling interval ... Floored at 2s - Afero rate-limits aggressively.' aioafero v1_const.py ships STATE_FETCH_FORBIDDEN_LIMIT=3 ('Consecutive per-device state GET 403s before pausing polls'), MAX_RETRIES=3, and DEFAULT_TOKEN_TIMEOUT=118s; jdeath README confirms a revoked session stays valid 'up to two more minutes' and that Hubspace has a 'Where You're Signed In' -> 'SIGN OUT OF ALL OTHER DEVICES' kill switch that does NOT auto-fire when MFA is enabled. aioafero implements OTPRequired/InvalidOTP flows (OTP support added Oct 2025), meaning MFA-enabled accounts challenge scripted logins — activation accounts should keep MFA off or pre-wire OTP handling. GitHub issue searches across both repos surface no 429-ban, lockout, or captcha reports; auth is standard Keycloak (realm 'thd') at accounts.hubspaceconnect.com with public OpenID discovery. Implication for the Canopy: one long-lived session per account with refresh-token reuse (aioafero's model) is the proven-quiet pattern; avoid repeated fresh password logins, which is the classic Keycloak brute-force trigger and the one thing nobody has publicly stress-tested.
  - src: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Expl0dingBanana/aioafero/main/src/aioafero/v1/v1_const.py
- [HIGH] **The community client works by impersonating the official Android app — client_id 'hubspace_android' plus a spoofed Android WebView User-Agent — which is both its main fingerprinting exposure and the optics problem in any 'we did nothing sneaky' narrative**
  - aioafero v1_const.py: AUTH_DEFAULT_CLIENT_ID='hubspace_android', AUTH_OPENID_HOST='accounts.hubspaceconnect.com', AUTH_REALM='thd', redirect 'hubspace-app://loginredirect', API hosts api2.afero.net and semantics2.afero.net, and DEFAULT_USERAGENT template 'Mozilla/5.0 (Linux; Android 15; ${client_name} Build/test; wv)...'. Afero can trivially distinguish real apps from bridges (TLS fingerprint, UA entropy, request cadence) whenever it chooses; it simply hasn't chosen to. For the activation this cuts two ways: (a) technical kill risk is a switch Afero already owns, so the Sep 23 bench gate should include a 'what if 403-everything' drill; (b) in a sanctioned-access pitch, proactively disclosing that today's plan-A rides the app's own OAuth client is cleaner than being discovered doing it during a co-branded event on THD property.
  - src: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Expl0dingBanana/aioafero/main/src/aioafero/v1/v1_const.py
- [HIGH] **DMCA 1201/CFAA landscape (commonly cited position, not legal advice): controlling devices you own through their intended cloud API with valid credentials sits in the most defensible corner of US interoperability law**
  - Chamberlain v. Skylink (Fed. Cir. 2004, 381 F.3d 1178) rejected a DMCA §1201 claim against an interoperable garage-door remote, requiring a nexus between circumvention and copyright infringement and noting buyers had implicit authorization to use interoperating products; it remains the anchor cite for third-party controllers of consumer devices. Van Buren v. United States (593 U.S. 178 (2021)) narrowed CFAA 'exceeds authorized access' to a gates-up-or-down test — using credentials you legitimately hold for a purpose the ToS dislikes is not a federal hacking crime (CRS LSB10616 summarizes). Logging into Keycloak with your own account and issuing documented REST calls circumvents no technological protection measure guarding a copyrighted work, so §1201 is largely inapposite; the residual exposure is contract (ToS breach -> account termination) and, theoretically, state-law claims. The posture worsens only if one bypasses an actual technical block (e.g., rotating credentials/IPs after a ban) — that is the line not to cross at a client activation.
  - src: https://www.eff.org/cases/chamberlain-group-inc-v-skylink-technologies-inc
- [HIGH] **Van Buren primary source for the CFAA half of the landscape paragraph**
  - Supreme Court slip opinion, June 3 2021, holding an individual 'exceeds authorized access' only by entering parts of a system that are off-limits to them, not by misusing access they legitimately have. Companion analysis: Congressional Research Service LSB10616 (congress.gov/crs-product/LSB10616). Justia case page: supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/593/19-783/.
  - src: https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-783_k53l.pdf
- [HIGH] **Sanctioned path exists and is concrete: Afero sells commercial platform access via negotiated Service Agreements, has a partnership intake page, and is financially intertwined with THD (Home Depot Ventures investor) — route the ask through the activation's existing THD client sponsor to the THD Hubspace merchandising team, in parallel with Afero's partnership contact**
  - Afero Developer ToS §2(a)/§3(d) codify the 'separate commercial service agreement' upsell; afero.io runs a 'Contact Afero | Let's Build Something Together' intake (afero.io/html/home/contact-afero.html) and markets itself as an enterprise IoT PaaS with a Home Depot case study (afero.io/html/home/case-study-home-depot.html); Home Depot Ventures announced an investment in Afero May 2022 (afero.io/html/home/article05.10.22.html); Afero press (BusinessWire, Dec 16 2025) announced 'Hubspace 2.0' — Afero remains the operating vendor. Legal notices: legal@afero.io (from the ToS). Realistic sequencing for an Oct 20 install: (1) THD client sponsor emails an authorization letter covering ~205 activation-owned devices and named accounts ('THD authorizes vendor X to programmatically control Hubspace devices owned by THD/agency for the House Beautiful activation, Oct 15-Nov 2 2026'); (2) that sponsor introduces Afero for either a blessed rate budget on the existing consumer API or a proper API arrangement. A signed Service Agreement in 4 weeks is unlikely; the authorization letter is the achievable artifact that converts ToS breach into licensed use, since HDPA/THD is itself the counterparty of the consumer ToU. There is no public evidence Afero sells self-serve API keys or has any public 'works with Hubspace' third-party program — everything is negotiated BD.
  - src: https://www.afero.io/html/home/contact-afero.html

  - BusinessWire release 'Afero Powers the Launch of Hubspace 2.0 Smart Home Platform' (Dec 16 2025; page 403s bots but is indexed) plus the Emkraan README's per-device availability feature motivated by devices 'vanishing from Afero's API during a Home-Depot-side backend migration'. jdeath issue #240 (July 2026) shows a transient fleet-wide 500 'Internal Server Error' on device control. - [HIGH] **Distribution without app-store friction is a solved problem for this activation: guest kiosk and staff iPads can run a PWA/web UI locked with Guided Access or supervised Single App Mode — zero Apple review, zero Apple program dependency**
  - The bridge already implies a local web server; serving the operator/guest UI as a full-screen web app and locking Safari (or a home-screen web app) via Guided Access (Settings > Accessibility, triple-click side button, passcode-protected: support.apple.com/en-us/111795) is the lowest-friction path. For hard kiosk behavior (survives reboot, disables hardware buttons, no escape without MDM), supervise the iPads with Apple Configurator and use Single App Mode (support.apple.com/guide/apple-configurator-2/single-app-mode-features-cadb1d640325 and the Apple Platform Deployment guide's supervised-restrictions chapter). This also neatly sidesteps App Review Guideline exposure for an app that drives a reverse-engineered API — nothing is submitted to Apple at all. The 10x20 canopy kiosk itself, if it's a PC/Android touchscreen, needs no Apple pathway (Chrome --kiosk / Windows Assigned Access).
  - src: https://support.apple.com/en-us/111795
- [HIGH] **If a native iOS operator app is wanted anyway: ad-hoc distribution (up to 100 registered devices per device family per membership year, no review) or TestFlight internal testing are the review-free lanes; unlisted and Custom Apps still pass App Review; the $299 Enterprise Program is ineligible and inappropriate for this use**
  - Apple Developer Program ($99/yr) supports ad-hoc provisioning to 100 registered UDIDs per product family per membership year (developer.apple.com/help/account/devices/devices-overview) — 4-6 activation iPads are trivially covered, profiles valid ~1 year, well past Nov 1 strike. TestFlight internal testing (up to 100 App Store Connect users) skips external Beta App Review. 'Unlisted app distribution' (link-only App Store) and Apple Business Manager Custom Apps both still undergo full App Review — friction and disclosure of the unofficial API. The Apple Developer Enterprise Program requires 100+ employees and internal-employee-only use; distributing an enterprise-signed app for a client activation is a program violation with account-ban risk (foresightmobile.com/blog/ios-app-distribution-guide-2026 summarizes current rules). Recommendation embedded in the evidence: PWA first; ad-hoc if native is forced; never enterprise certs.
  - src: https://developer.apple.com/help/account/devices/devices-overview
- [HIGH] **Trademark care: HUBSPACE is a live USPTO-registered mark of Home Depot Product Authority, LLC, THD's ToU promises 'aggressive' IP enforcement and forbids using THD marks in publicity without prior written permission — so 'works with Hubspace' phrasing survives only as disciplined nominative fair use, and ideally under the activation's written brand approval instead**
  - USPTO records: HUBSPACE serials 88128659 (filed 2018), 88774500, 90475268, plus HUBSPACE CONNECT serial 99163808 filed Apr 30 2025, all owned by Home Depot Product Authority, LLC (trademarks.justia.com/881/28/hubspace-88128659.html). The Hubspace App ToU states THD 'aggressively enforces its intellectual property rights' and bars use of THD names/logos 'in advertising or publicity' without prior written permission. Nominative fair use (New Kids on the Block test, codified in 9th Cir. Model Civil Jury Instructions §15.24: ce9.uscourts.gov/jury-instructions/print/book/export/html/251) permits truthful compatibility references if (1) the product isn't identifiable without the mark, (2) only as much of the mark as necessary is used (words only — never the Hubspace logo, colorway, or stylization), (3) nothing suggests sponsorship — e.g. 'Compatible with Hubspace® devices. Not affiliated with or endorsed by The Home Depot or Afero.' Inside this co-branded activation the cleaner instrument is a line item in the SOW/brand-approval chain licensing the mark for event UI; the nominative-fair-use posture matters mainly for the AFTERWARD scenario — pitching the bespoke app to other clients or publicly — where the disclaimer plus no-logo rule is mandatory and the Afero Developer ToS no-publicity clause (if ever signed) adds a consent requirement.
  - src: https://trademarks.justia.com/881/28/hubspace-88128659.html
- [MEDIUM] **The 'bespoke replacement app' pitched commercially after the activation carries a different, heavier risk profile than the activation itself — it converts tolerated interop into a distributed commercial product built on an unlicensed API**
  - Activation: THD is the client, devices/accounts are owned, use is on-site, short-lived, and effectively sanctioned — contract risk approaches zero with a sponsor letter. Productized app: distribution to third parties of a client that commercializes Afero's platform squarely conflicts with the consumer ToU ('personal, noncommercial'), would need App Review (Guideline 5.2.2/4.x exposure for unauthorized third-party service use has sunk similar unofficial clients), invites the platform-owner response that hobbyist HA integrations never provoked, and competes with Afero's own paid deliverable (they built and ship the official app — the Google Play package is literally io.afero.partner.hubspace). The defensible commercial pitch is the one aimed AT the client: a THD/Afero-licensed operator/retail experience layer, sold with their API blessing — evidenced feasible by Afero's Service Agreement structure. Pitch it as 'we'll build it on your licensed API' not 'we built it on your reverse-engineered API'.
  - src: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=io.afero.partner.hubspace&hl=en_US

## Bench tests
- Login-churn probe on a sacrificial Hubspace account: perform 5, 15, then 30 fresh password OAuth logins (PKCE flow via accounts.hubspaceconnect.com realm 'thd') within one hour from the venue IP; record at which count, if any, Keycloak returns temporary-lockout/'invalid credentials' on valid creds, an OTP challenge appears, or a security email arrives. This determines whether the 5-account architecture can survive worst-case bridge restarts during show hours.
- Rate-limit ceiling measurement: with one account and ~40 real bulbs, ramp sustained request rate (state GETs + state PUTs) from 0.5 to 20 rps and log the first appearance of 429s or 403s from api2.afero.net, plus whether the throttle is per-account, per-token, or per-IP (test two accounts from one IP vs one account from two IPs). Output: a hard requests/sec budget per zone for the 200-bulb canopy.
- Concurrent-session coexistence: hold the bridge's refresh-token session while logging the same account into the official Hubspace app on a staff phone; verify neither evicts the other over 24h, then trigger 'Manage Account > Where You're Signed In > SIGN OUT OF ALL OTHER DEVICES' and time bridge death (expected <=2 min) and scripted recovery — this is the documented kill switch an operator could fat-finger.
- MFA/OTP posture check: confirm all 5 activation accounts have MFA disabled and that aioafero never raises OTPRequired across 50 cold re-authentications spread over 48h (including 3 AM runs); separately enable MFA on the sacrificial account to capture exactly what the OTP challenge looks like so operators recognize it.
- Security-notification watch: monitor the inboxes of all activation accounts during the full soak week for 'new device sign-in' or anomaly emails triggered by the spoofed Android-WebView user agent from a datacenter-looking venue IP; zero emails = quiet fingerprint, any email = document and pre-brief client.
- 8-hour soak with denial telemetry: during the standard soak, log every non-200 from api2.afero.net/semantics2.afero.net by device ID and status code, specifically watching for the per-device 403 pattern aioafero's STATE_FETCH_FORBIDDEN_LIMIT was built for; any organic 403/429 during normal-rate operation changes the risk narrative and belongs in the Sep 23 go/no-go.
- Ban-response fire drill: simulate a total API denial (null-route api2.afero.net at the router) mid-scene and verify the kiosk degrades gracefully (holds last scene, shows no error to guests) and that the team's plan-B decision tree (who calls whom, Art-Net fallback trigger) executes; an actual Afero-side block would look exactly like this.
